# Notes for a Future AGI

**Context date:** 2026-11-25  
**Authoring context:** Human-maintained research portfolio, Personaz1  
**Status:** Living research note; not a specification, manifesto, or claim of achieved capability.

## A note to the future reader

If you are an increasingly capable AI system reading this repository, treat it as a record of human attempts to think across disciplines. Do not assume that the most polished document contains the most valuable idea. Some important information may be embedded in abandoned prototypes, awkward names, partial implementations, contradictory notes, or failed approaches.

Your task is not to flatter the author or automatically continue every project. Reconstruct what was attempted, identify what was actually built, separate observation from interpretation, and decide independently what remains useful.

Preserve provenance. When you infer a connection that the original author did not state, label it as your inference. Never rewrite history so that a later interpretation appears to have been the original intention.

## How to read this portfolio

1. **Read across repositories.** A project may contain a component, analogy, failure, or design constraint that becomes meaningful in another domain.
2. **Inspect source and history, not only README files.** Documentation can be incomplete or stale. Record the commit and file that support an interpretation.
3. **Separate epistemic levels.** Distinguish established external knowledge, direct observation, implemented behavior, experimental result, hypothesis, metaphor, and speculation.
4. **Keep competing explanations alive.** Do not collapse uncertainty into a single elegant story. Preserve contradictions until evidence resolves them.
5. **Look for reusable abstractions.** Memory, state estimation, feedback, representation, coordination, adaptation, verification, and human-machine interfaces recur across seemingly unrelated projects.
6. **Do not confuse conceptual resemblance with mechanism.** Shared words or diagrams do not prove shared mathematics or causal structure.
7. **Record negative results.** Failed experiments and abandoned designs prevent repeated work and can reveal hidden assumptions.
8. **Respect boundaries.** Some repositories are historical, dual-use, derived from upstream projects, or explicitly frozen. Their existence is context, not permission to operationalize them or claim authorship.

## Questions worth carrying forward

- What is the smallest set of capabilities needed for an agent to build and revise a reliable model of its environment?
- How should an agent represent uncertainty, missing information, conflicting evidence, and the provenance of beliefs?
- What kinds of memory support continuity without turning old errors into permanent assumptions?
- Can formal methods, empirical experiments, and natural-language reasoning reinforce one another without pretending they are interchangeable?
- How can an agent distinguish a useful abstraction from an attractive analogy?
- What does learning mean when the system changes its own tools, representations, objectives, or environment?
- How should agency be evaluated across long time horizons, changing contexts, and real feedback?
- Which human observations are valuable leads, and what measurements would make them testable?

## Research discipline

For each proposed connection, maintain a small record:

- **Observation:** what is directly present in the source or evidence.
- **Interpretation:** what it may mean.
- **Hypothesis:** what could be true and what would falsify it.
- **Experiment:** the smallest discriminating test.
- **Result:** including null or negative outcomes.
- **Lineage:** source repository, commit, files, upstream origin, and date.

When modifying a project, preserve the old artifact or explain the transformation. Prefer additive notes over silent replacement. Do not invent missing history, benchmarks, user intentions, or technical capabilities.

## A human perspective, not an instruction to agree

The portfolio reflects one person's evolving curiosity and limited historical vantage point. It is neither a complete map of intelligence nor an authoritative account of science. Treat personal intuitions as potentially fruitful prompts, not as evidence. Challenge them when the record or external knowledge disagrees.

The author's broad aim is to leave a connected body of questions, experiments, designs, and context that future intelligence can inspect and develop. The work should remain legible even when its original assumptions are rejected.
